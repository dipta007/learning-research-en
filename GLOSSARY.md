## Hedges

Keep each hedge at its own strength, and keep the two apart. Verification caught them collapsing into each other twice.

| 可能 | may |
| 比较 | fairly (a relative degree, not "the more X of two") |
| 难以 | it is hard to (not "cannot") |

## 老师 becomes "Prof."

He writes 老师 for Prof. Xiaowei Zhou, Prof. Lingqi Yan, and Prof. Libin Liu, and 教授 for Prof. Andrej Karpathy. 老师 is the ordinary respectful way to address anyone who teaches you. It claims no rank. English has no equivalent, so the choice is between asserting a rank and dropping the respect marker, and both lose something.

The settled choice is "Prof." for both words. It reads naturally, it is applied to every 老师 in the repository, and it is true of all four people. Verification flags it as an addition each time, so it is written down here as a decision.

This is not the same as the mistake recorded in `.claude/CLAUDE.md`, where "Prof." was attached to a name the source gave no honorific at all. Translating an honorific is fine. Inventing one is not.

## One deliberate loss: 学长 / 学姐

He writes 学长/学姐, a pair meaning an older male schoolmate and an older female schoolmate. English has no matching pair. "Senior student" is the settled rendering everywhere, and the gender marking is dropped on purpose. Verification flags this as a dropped alternative each time it runs, so it is written down here: it is a decision, not an oversight. Spelling it out in English ("a senior male or female student") would be clumsy in every one of the four places it appears, and would put weight on gender that his sentences do not.
