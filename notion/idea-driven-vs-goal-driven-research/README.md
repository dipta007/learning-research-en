# Idea-driven research vs. Goal-driven research

> [Original Article](https://pengsida.notion.site/d9c6556326e84962a2d7ae190e2705af)

What idea-driven research is: make X work better.

> **note**
> Choose a direction → see a paper → think of an idea, so as to do better than that paper.

What goal-driven research is: work for a goal.

> **note** Settle on a goal you want to reach (new AI capabilities)
> → plan a roadmap for reaching that goal (a set of important tasks)
> → complete the tasks one by one, and update your roadmap as you go.

<details>
<summary>The advantages and disadvantages of idea-driven research</summary>

Advantage: it is easy to get started with. This is probably how most beginners do research.

Disadvantages:

1. Because you are following someone else, the paper's impact is limited unless the results are a good deal better.
2. It is easy to end up with the same idea as someone else, because other people may also be improving that same paper.
3. Because some solutions already exist, the room for innovation in the solution is limited. You need to be very clever and to have a deep insight to propose a breakthrough new technique for that task, which is fairly difficult.

</details>

<details>
<summary>The advantages and disadvantages of goal-driven research</summary>

Advantages:

1. It is easier to produce a breakthrough innovation, because you are no longer a follow-up but a leader.
2. Your own research has more motivation and a big picture, which makes it easier to attract other people.
3. The research is more continuous and forms a coherent body.
4. You can organise a team, with people solving the tasks on the roadmap separately.

Disadvantages:

1. Planning a good roadmap is a very hard thing to do.
2. It is easy to end up with a task-specific technique. You need to require that the technique you propose is as general as possible, so it can be used in other fields.

</details>
